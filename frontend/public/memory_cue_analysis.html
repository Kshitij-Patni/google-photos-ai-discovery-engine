Memory Cue Analysis

This report helps us understand exactly how users try to remember and search for their photos.

When a user opens Google Photos to find an old picture, they usually rely on a "Memory Cue". This could be:

  • Visual: "I remember she was wearing a red shirt."
  • Temporal: "I know I took it last summer."
  • Spatial: "It was taken at the beach in Florida."
  • People: "It was a photo of John and Mary."

Key Findings

  1. Visual Cues are heavily used, but often fail. Users expect the app to find photos based on visual descriptions (like "dog in snow"), but the search engine frequently fails to match these complex visual memories.
  2. Temporal Cues are essential for Events. When users look for birthdays, weddings, or vacations, they almost always scroll to a specific month or year. If the app's timeline gets out of order, they get extremely frustrated.
  3. Users don't think in "Keywords". While our app requires users to type search queries, users actually think in terms of stories and relationships. Forcing them to guess the exact tags the AI assigned to a photo causes friction.
